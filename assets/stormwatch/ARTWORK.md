# Stormwatch artwork

The refuge image is original generated artwork based on the user's approved
Stormwatch concept: a warm lighthouse keeper's room above a violent coastal storm.
It was created with the built-in image generation tool on 6 November 2026.

The production plate uses this editing prompt:
"Preserve the exact camera, layout, crop, every architectural edge, window mullion,
stove frame, furniture, mug, lighting and all texture detail. Remove the distant
lightning bolt and its local white glow, filling with the same dark storm clouds;
remove all steam above the coffee mug, reconstructing the sill and stone behind
it; remove all droplets and rain streaks on the window glass; remove just the
bright tall flames inside the stove while preserving the orange glowing logs
and ember bed and the stove door metal dividers. Keep the storm, rough sea,
rocks and wave foam, the room, lamp and all warm light otherwise unchanged."

`refuge.webp` is the selected production plate, converted to WebP without
recomposition. `surfaces.png` is numeric shader data generated by
`verification/prepare-stormwatch-masks.py`, not a second illustration.

The room and distant sky use projected artwork on depth layers. From v1.18.1,
the sea is displaced 3D geometry; the breakwater, logs and coals are solid meshes;
the fire is a raymarched 3D volume; and spray follows 3D particle trajectories.
No water or flame photograph is sampled by those effects. The scene offers a
stationary seated perspective with bounded pan and zoom.

From v1.18.2 the sea uses a predominantly aligned swell spectrum, with advected
seeded fields bending and spacing the crests and changing their height. Long
crests extend across the visible horizon. Per-wavelength distance filtering
suppresses under-resolved geometry instead of letting it alias into a grid.
Foam appears in irregular crest patches; spray responds to actual local crests.

v1.18.3 increases swell amplitude and whitewater, and adds stronger wind-driven
spray at coastal impacts and offshore whitecaps while retaining crest alignment.
Foam fragments are independent, surface-following quads that spawn on crests,
drift and fade. Airborne splash droplets follow wind and gravity trajectories.

`firelight.webp` preserves the original approved render as a secondary projection
texture for the explicitly labelled still fallback only. The animated fire does
not load or sample this image.
