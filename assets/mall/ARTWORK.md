# Mall atrium artwork

The scene follows the user's approved circa-1990 mall concept, with Side A Records, Rewind Video, The Book Nook, Galaxy Arcade, a bakery and an ice-cream shop.

- `still.webp`: format conversion of the exact selected reference, `image-edit-target-7ff352ef9cac7234.png` (1672 × 941). Used for the background thumbnail and the explicitly labelled fallback when WebGL cannot open.
- `atrium.webp`: a clean-plate edit made with OpenAI image generation on 7 November 2026, using that selected reference. Only the fountain water and spray were cleared for live compositing; the architecture, shops and seated composition were preserved. Output was 1671 × 941 and is registered to the same normalized image bounds.
- `surfaces.png`: numerical RGB registration data generated by `verification/prepare-mall-masks.py`. Red marks the basin opening, green retains the old tread registration (unused since v1.19.3), and blue marks CRT screens.
- `escalators.png` (v1.19.3): numeric RGB registration generated by `verification/prepare-mall-escalators.py`. Red and green delimit the visible up/down step surfaces; blue identifies moving belt surfaces. Foreground palms, balustrades and landing surrounds are excluded.
- `liquid-flow.webp` (v1.19.2): 144 distinct transparent frames of connected 3D water meshes, rendered locally in Blender 4.3.2 / Cycles. Seeded emission tracks vary pressure and launch direction; gravity carries particles upward and back down. Density surfaces are reconstructed for every frame, with independent short splash trajectories near the return area. The six-second cycle is periodic and interpolated at runtime. No stock footage or generated still plume is used.


The architecture is generated artwork, not a surveyed reconstruction of a real mall. Its small poster and product details are decorative. It is projected from a fixed seat with bounded pan/zoom. The basin surface is displaced live geometry. The jets and their droplets use the pre-rendered water sequence on registered planes, with mirrored animation for reflections. The escalator treads and risers are moving 3D faces, clipped to the fixed seated composition; handrail grain and joints move on registered belt surfaces. CRT activity remains a restrained surface effect. No characters, downloaded film trailers, audio, external APIs or runtime image-generation service are used.

The water model is a ballistic approximation, not a pressure-solving fluid simulation. It is baked for the fixed seated viewpoint; it does not support walking around the fountain. The app performs no fluid solve and requires no external account. Each jet uses a separate time offset and a height-adjusted playback rate. The old warped plume still and extra glitter-like particle layer were removed.

Rebuild the asset (offline development work, not required to use the app):

1. Install Python with NumPy, SciPy and Pillow, and Blender 4.3.2 with Cycles.
2. From the repository root, run `python3 verification/bake-mall-water-particles.py`.
3. Run `blender --background --threads 8 --python verification/render-mall-water.py`.
4. Run `python3 verification/pack-mall-water.py`.

The intermediate meshes and frames go to `mall-water-bake/`; set `DAYCRAFT_WATER_BAKE` to use another directory. The final atlas is 1536 × 2304 pixels, about 1.47 MB on disk and 13.5 MiB of GPU RGBA texture memory. Mipmaps are disabled to avoid neighboring animation frames bleeding together.
